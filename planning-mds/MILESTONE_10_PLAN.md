# Milestone 10 Plan — Examples, Docs, and Release Governance

This plan defines how the 85-contract canonical surface plus the Fabric target are made consumable, navigable, and governable. It is the detailed companion to **Milestone 10** in `planning-mds/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md` and to the M10 work items added to `planning-mds/STATUS.md`.

This document is authoritative for Milestone 10. Generator scripts, tracked docs, and contract-status edits follow what this plan specifies; if they diverge, this plan is the source of truth and the artifacts must be regenerated or re-edited.

**Precondition:** this plan assumes the canonical surface is at 0.4.x post-hardening (Milestone 8.5, W025–W031, complete) and the Fabric target is fully shipped (Milestone 9, W023.F1–F8, complete). M10.1–M10.6 do not start until both are in place.

Last updated: 2026-05-07.

## 1. Intent

Bring the repository to a state where:

- A new contributor can follow a worked end-to-end walkthrough on a non-policy area and understand the canonical-to-Fabric flow without reading every file.
- A consumer can read one tracked inventory page and find every canonical contract by kind, subject area, version, and status.
- A consumer can pin against a release and see, at a glance, what changed since the previous pin.
- A reviewer can run a documented checklist against an incoming PR and decide whether it meets the canonical-layer bar.
- The versioning story extends past the canonical contract surface to the Fabric manifest surface: a contract bump produces a predictable manifest / DDL / Purview / notebook regen, and the consumer-side pinning rules are written down.
- Mature contracts carry a status that reflects the work invested in them — every contract advances at least to `proposed`, and a reviewed cohort advances to `approved`.

M10's output is a repository that is finished as a deliverable, not just as an authoring sandbox.

### Success criteria

- One additional worked-example walkthrough lives under `targets/fabric/examples/` covering a non-policy area that exercises the append-only event family, the financial-transaction family, and the C4.5 commercial-lines spine. The walkthrough mirrors `end-to-end-policy.md` in shape and depth.
- A tracked contract-inventory page lives under `docs/` (regenerated by a script under `scripts/generation/`) and surfaces all 85 contracts with kind, subject area, version, status, and a one-line description.
- A repo-level `CHANGELOG.md` aggregates per-contract `customProperties.changelog` entries and is regenerated by a script. Entries are grouped by canonical version wave (0.1.x → 0.4.x) and named at the contract granularity.
- `docs/review-checklist.md` codifies the canonical-validator + Fabric-drift validator + C7 cleanup checks into a PR-review checklist organized by contract surface area.
- `references/design-decisions/pc/versioning-policy.md` extends to cover the manifest-version surface (`*.fabric.yaml` `manifestVersion`), Fabric artifact regeneration cadence, and consumer-side pinning guidance.
- All 85 contracts move from `status: draft` to `status: proposed`. A documented cohort (policy area, plus the codesets the policy area depends on) advances further to `status: approved`. Held-back contracts have a written reason in this plan.

## 2. Non-Negotiable Boundaries

The following are constraints. The implementation must obey them; if a phase pulls in tension with one, the phase is wrong, not the constraint.

- **Source-neutral framing.** No tracked artifact (this plan, walkthroughs, inventory, changelog, checklist, ADR amendments) names external reference models, source URLs, source-document identifiers, copied class hierarchies, or scratch mappings. Findings stay framed as canonical-modeling decisions.
- **Canonical contracts remain the source of truth.** M10 does not edit canonical contract field shape or semantics. The only contract edits in scope are `status:` transitions (M10.6) and the corresponding changelog entries.
- **No version churn for documentation deliverables.** M10.1–M10.5 produce tracked docs and one extension to an ADR. None of those bumps any contract version.
- **Status-promotion bumps follow `versioning-policy.md`.** A status change to a contract whose schema does not change is a `PATCH` bump (`x.y.Z`). Promoting `pc.policy` from 0.4.2 `draft` to `proposed` produces 0.4.3 `proposed`. Promoting from `proposed` to `approved` produces another patch. Bulk per-cohort changes still bump per contract; M10.6 does not invent a bulk-bump shortcut.
- **No target work pulled forward.** Additional targets (Databricks, Snowflake, Kafka, semantic projection) remain deferred per `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md` §"Deferred Scope". M10's manifest-version rules apply to the Fabric target only.
- **Generators remain idempotent.** New scripts under `scripts/generation/` (inventory, changelog) follow the same idempotent posture as the four Fabric generators: rerun produces no diff if no inputs changed.
- **The canonical validator stays the contract layer's gate.** M10.4's review checklist documents the validators and the C7 cleanup pattern but does not duplicate them. A reviewer always reaches for the validators first.

### 3.3 Status-promotion strategy

The current state, found by inspecting the contract files:

- **All 85 contracts are at `status: draft`.** STATUS.md and IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md narrate "proposed" because W019 (validator) added `proposed` to the allowed status list, but no later phase actually flipped contract files. M10.6 is the first phase to change contract `status:` values en masse.
- Every contract has at least one `customProperties.changelog` entry (W027 onward), so the `draft → proposed` validator-enforceable gate passes mechanically across the whole surface.
- Every relationship's `targetContractId` resolves (C1.3 confirmed); every `*_code` field is codeset-bound or `codesetExempt: true` (C5); every contract has at least one error-severity quality rule. Those are the validator-enforceable `proposed → approved` gates from `status-promotion.md` §"proposed → approved".

So the surface is mechanically clean for `draft → proposed` end-to-end, and mechanically clean for `proposed → approved` for most contracts. The constraint on `proposed → approved` is the **process-enforced** gates: steward sign-off, known consumers, all referenced codesets at least `proposed`. M10.6 is bounded by what we can credibly assert about those process gates today.

The plan therefore splits status promotion into two cohorts:

| Cohort | Target status | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **All 85 contracts** | `draft → proposed` | Validator-enforceable gates pass everywhere; no process gate stops the cohort transition. Promotion to `proposed` simply records that the contracts have been authored, refactored, and validated against the strengthened rule set. |
| **Policy + supporting reference data + product** | `proposed → approved` | The policy area is the worked example in F7. The four contracts in `targets/fabric/examples/end-to-end-policy.md` plus their codesets are the only contracts with a tracked downstream consumer (the worked example itself). Approval is anchored on that consumer. |

Other contracts hold at `proposed`. The "held back" reasons land in this plan §6.

### Phase M10.3 — Repo-level CHANGELOG

**Goal:** aggregate the per-contract `customProperties.changelog` entries into a single repository-level `CHANGELOG.md` that consumers can read to answer "what changed since I last pinned."

Deliverables:

- `scripts/generation/generate-changelog.py` — reads `references/odcs/pc/**/*.odcs.yaml`, emits `CHANGELOG.md` at the repository root. Idempotent.
- `CHANGELOG.md` — generated artifact at the repo root. Sections:
  1. Header explaining what the file is, how it is generated, and how to regenerate (one paragraph).
  2. Per-version-wave entries, newest first. Versions are parsed from each per-contract changelog string and grouped: `0.4.x`, `0.3.x`, `0.2.x`, `0.1.x` (the original ship state).
  3. Within each wave, a table with columns: contract id, version, date (parsed from the changelog string when present), summary (the changelog entry verbatim, leading version-prefix stripped).
  4. A short "How to read this file" footer pointing at `versioning-policy.md` and `status-promotion.md`.

Generator behavior:

- Each per-contract changelog entry follows the convention used in C3–C7: `'<version>: <summary> (<author>, <YYYY-MM-DD>).'`. The generator parses by leading-`<version>:` token; entries that do not match are emitted with "—" in date and "(parse failed)" appended to the summary, surfacing rather than hiding malformed entries.
- A regen banner identical to M10.2's lives at the top of the file.
- Output is sorted by version (descending), then contract id (ascending) within a version.
- The generator does **not** consult git history; the source of truth for what changed in each wave is the contract's own changelog entries. (If a future milestone wants release-notes that draw on commit messages or PR titles, that is a separate generator.)

Acceptance criteria:

- Every per-contract changelog entry across all 85 contracts is reflected in `CHANGELOG.md`.
- Rerun produces no diff.
- The version waves match what STATUS.md and IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md narrate (0.4.x final, 0.3.x bulk refactor, 0.2.0 ADR-driven refactor, 0.1.x originals).
- The file is reachable from the root README "What's here" / "Modeling References" section.

Estimated scope: one Python script (~200 lines), one generated markdown file (size depends on changelog density — likely ~600–900 lines).

### Phase M10.4 — PR review checklist

**Goal:** give a reviewer a single tracked page that codifies what the validators check and what the C7 single-contract cleanups taught us, structured by contract surface area.

Deliverables:

- `docs/review-checklist.md` — single new authored file. Sections:
  1. **Pre-review automation.** What the reviewer should expect to be green before opening the PR: `validate-contracts.py` (zero findings); `validate-fabric-manifests.py --require-full-coverage` (when canonical contracts are touched, manifests must regenerate cleanly); `pytest scripts/validation/tests/` (40 tests, all green).
  2. **By contract kind.** For each of entity / event / transaction / codeset, a checklist of the structural and semantic checks a reviewer makes that the validators do not.
     - Entity: PK is `*_uid`; SCD2 fields present; `record_status_code` present; classifications make sense; codeset bindings present on every `*_code`; relationships explicit; quality rules at error severity for the must-be-true invariants; description names the canonical concept, not the source-system shape.
     - Event: append-only mode; `correction_indicator` + `corrects_*_uid` paired; `event_datetime` present; partitioning by `event_datetime`; no SCD2 fields; no `created_datetime` / `updated_datetime` (per C1.5).
     - Transaction: append-only; `lifecycle_event_uid` cross-reference if relevant; currency pairing on amounts; classification membership in `pc.financial-transaction-classification` if applicable.
     - Codeset: `code_value` field, `code_label` field, classification profile `PUBLIC` for pure codesets and `INTERNAL` for richer reference-data entities; no `_uid` foreign keys back into entity contracts (codesets are not joined to entities through their internal `*_uid`).
  3. **Cross-cutting checks.** ADR back-links present (`customProperties.adrs: [...]`); changelog entry on every version bump; classification profile matches max field sensitivity; narrative free-text classified `CONFIDENTIAL + PII` unless `narrativeException`.
  4. **Status-promotion review.** When the PR changes `status:`, what to verify per the gate it crosses (mirroring `status-promotion.md` §"Promotion gates").
  5. **Fabric impact.** When canonical contract field shape changes, what regenerates and what to inspect (the manifest digest, DDL diff, Purview JSON diff, run-summary changes that downstream notebooks will produce). Cross-link to `targets/fabric/examples/end-to-end-policy.md` §9 and `targets/fabric/examples/end-to-end-claims.md` §9 for the worked traces.
  6. **Source-neutrality boundary.** Tracked files do not name external reference models, source URLs, source-document identifiers, copied class hierarchies, or scratch mappings (per `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md` §"Non-Negotiable Boundaries").
- Cross-link to the new file from `docs/authoring-guide.md` and from the root README.

Acceptance criteria:

- Every check on the page either points at a validator that already enforces it, or names a specific reason a human reviewer must judge it (with criteria).
- A reviewer who follows the page in order produces a deterministic decision: pass or named blocker.
- The page references each ADR by id and resolves cleanly under `references/design-decisions/pc/`.

Estimated scope: one document, ~300–400 lines.

### Phase M10.5 — Versioning and compatibility rules

**Goal:** extend `versioning-policy.md` past the canonical contract surface to cover the manifest version surface, Fabric artifact regeneration cadence, and consumer-side pinning rules.

Deliverables:

- Edit `references/design-decisions/pc/versioning-policy.md` to add three new sections:
  - **Manifest version (`*.fabric.yaml`).** The `manifestVersion: 1.0.0` field at the top of every Fabric manifest is the schema version of the manifest format itself, independent of the source contract version. A manifest schema change (e.g. a new role in §5.2 of `FABRIC_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`, a new `qualityRules.type`, a change to the role taxonomy) bumps `manifestVersion` per the same SemVer rules as canonical contracts. Manifest consumers (the Fabric notebooks) declare a compatible `manifestVersion` range; the manifest validator enforces.
  - **Fabric artifact regeneration cadence.** A canonical contract bump produces, in lockstep, regenerated manifest, DDL, Purview JSON, and run-summary surface. The manifest validator's digest pin makes this deterministic: every contract bump that touches schema produces a new manifest digest; missing regen is surfaced by the validator. Notebook templates regenerate only when the notebook generator changes, never per-contract — they read manifests at runtime. Three rules:
    1. PATCH bumps to a contract regenerate manifest (digest), DDL (table comment), Purview JSON (`sourceContractVersion` strings). Notebooks unchanged.
    2. MINOR bumps regenerate the same files plus column-level changes wherever a column was added, plus glossary terms when descriptions changed. Notebooks unchanged.
    3. MAJOR bumps trigger the same regen plus a deliberate review of any consumer pinning, since some consumers will break. The manifest validator does not by itself catch consumer-pinning impact — it only catches drift within the file artifact bundle.
  - **Consumer-side pinning guidance.** Consumers (e.g. Fabric workspaces, downstream Gold-layer ETL, AI/RAG indexes that ingest the contracts) pin against a canonical contract version, a manifest version, or a repository tag. Three pinning patterns:
    1. **Pin a contract version.** A consumer that depends on a specific contract (e.g. `pc.policy@0.4.2`) pins on the `version:` field. The pin is broken when MAJOR bumps that contract, signaled by the consumer's CI failing the validator's version-comparison check.
    2. **Pin a manifest version.** A consumer of the Fabric manifest format (e.g. a custom downstream notebook) pins on `manifestVersion`. Independent of contract pins.
    3. **Pin a repo tag.** A consumer that wants the entire bundle (contracts + manifests + DDL + Purview + notebooks + walkthroughs) pins a git tag. Most production deployments use this — it is the simplest path to reproducibility.
  - One paragraph naming what is **not** in scope of versioning-policy: workspace-level Fabric versions (Lakehouse runtime, Spark version, Delta version) — those are platform concerns owned by the deployer.
- Cross-link the new sections to `targets/fabric/manifest-schema.md` (manifest schema reference) and `scripts/validation/validate-fabric-manifests.py` (drift validator).

Acceptance criteria:

- `versioning-policy.md` reads as a coherent whole — the canonical-layer SemVer rules and the manifest-layer rules use the same vocabulary and do not contradict each other.
- The C2 reconciliation principle still applies: the ADR matches what shipped. The shipped reality is `manifestVersion: 1.0.0` everywhere; the ADR documents that as the starting point.
- No contract version is bumped by this phase. ADR edits are governance.

Estimated scope: ~150 lines added to `versioning-policy.md`.

## 5. Open Questions and Pending Decisions

1. **Should the held-back claims cohort advance to `approved` in a follow-up milestone (M10.7) once the M10.1 walkthrough has settled, or is the next promotion wave deferred to M11?** Current default: defer to a future milestone, not in this plan's scope.
2. **Should the contract inventory page (M10.2) include change-detection metadata (diff against last release tag)?** Current default: no — keep it a navigation surface, let `CHANGELOG.md` (M10.3) own the change-detection narrative. Reconsider if a consumer asks.
3. **Does `versioning-policy.md` need a separate ADR for the manifest version, or is an extension to the existing ADR cleaner?** Current default: extend the existing ADR. The manifest version is a derivative of the canonical contract surface and belongs in the same vocabulary doc.
4. **Should the M10.4 review checklist also cover Fabric manifest authoring (it shouldn't — manifests are generated)?** Yes, but only as a one-line "manifests are not authored; if a manifest needs editing, the generator is wrong" pointer.
5. **Should there be an `M10.7 — release tag and version-bump dry run`?** Considered and rejected for this milestone. Tagging cadence is a consumer-side decision; the repo is ready to be tagged but the tag itself is a separate operation.

## 7. Out of Scope for This Milestone

Per the boundary established in `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md` §"Deferred Scope", the following are explicitly out of scope for M10:

- **Risk-transfer contract family.** Reinsurance, coinsurance, fronting, self-insurance — deferred per `risk-transfer-scope.md` (W021).
- **Semantic projection.** RDF / OWL / SKOS / knowledge graph derivation — deferred per W009.
- **Targets beyond Fabric.** Databricks, Snowflake, Kafka, API targets — deferred. M10.5's manifest-version rules apply to the Fabric target only.
- **`review` status reintroduction.** C2 dropped `review` from the validator allowlist. If a future milestone wants `review` back, the change goes through `status-promotion.md` first, then the validator. Not in M10.
- **Promotion to `1.0.0`.** A contract may be `approved` while at `0.x` (per `versioning-policy.md`). Promotion to `1.0.0` requires a real production consumer pinning to the contract; that is a downstream event, not an M10 milestone deliverable.
- **Production release tagging.** The repo is ready to be tagged but the tag itself is a consumer-driven operation, not part of M10.
- **Live release-notes generation from git.** `CHANGELOG.md` (M10.3) is generated from per-contract `customProperties.changelog`, not from commit messages. A future generator could draw on git history; that is a separate piece of tooling.
- **Cross-area worked examples (e.g. claims + policy + financial in one walkthrough).** Out of scope; M10.1 is one new walkthrough, not three.

## 8. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| M10.1 walkthrough drifts from real append-only / transaction notebook behavior | Mirror `end-to-end-policy.md` §6.1's structured-stub posture; cross-check the run-summary JSON against `SHARED_RUN_SUMMARY_SOURCE` in `generate-fabric-notebooks.py`. |
| Inventory page (M10.2) becomes stale | Generated, not authored; banner at the top names the script; rerun produces no diff so any drift is a CI failure. |
| `CHANGELOG.md` (M10.3) misses entries due to malformed changelog strings in contracts | Generator surfaces parse failures rather than hiding them ("(parse failed)" appended); reviewer fixes the source contract entry. |
| Review checklist (M10.4) duplicates the validators and rots | Each item points at the validator that enforces it where one exists; only items that genuinely require human judgment get prose. |
| Versioning extension (M10.5) contradicts the canonical-layer rules | Same SemVer vocabulary applied at both the canonical and manifest layers; consequences section enumerated for both. |
| Status-promotion edits (M10.6) regress validator | Refactor script reruns the validator after each contract edit; aborts on first failure; idempotent on rerun. |
| Cohort-promotion choice gets second-guessed mid-execution | Cohort decision is the pause-for-review checkpoint 2; once approved, edits are mechanical. |
| STATUS.md / IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md narrative inconsistency persists | M10.6 explicitly corrects both files as part of its closeout. |
| `manifestVersion` regen contract is ambiguous after M10.5 | The validator's drift checks already enforce digest pinning; M10.5 documents the contract that already exists rather than introducing a new gate. |
